## D — the question

> What are five years of a company's statements worth, built from assumptions you can defend,
> and how do you know the statements are right?

The second clause is the one this lab actually turns on. Producing five years of statements is
arithmetic. Knowing they are right requires a check that can fail, and a model that stops when
it does.

## I — the engine

[`proforma.py`](../proforma.py), repository root, standard library only. Run with
`python proforma.py`.

The order of operations is the design. Each year computes the income statement, then every
balance sheet line **except** cash, then free cash flow to equity, and only then cash. Two
conventions worth naming:

- **Depreciation and interest are charged on opening balances.** Assets bought during the year
  have not been in service a full year, and money borrowed during the year was not outstanding
  for all of it.
- **Floor plan is treated as operating, not financing.** It funds inventory, so an inventory
  build the lender finances should not read as a cash drain.

The valuation discounts the five FCFE at the cost of equity and adds a terminal value of
`(2030 FCFE + 2030 repayment) × (1 + g) ÷ (Ke − g)`, discounted five years. The repayment is
added back because a scheduled paydown is not a perpetual claim on cash — capitalising FCFE net
of repayment would assume the company pays down debt forever and would understate the terminal.

## The model refuses a broken balance sheet

`assert_balanced()` runs **before** `value_equity()` and raises on either of two checks: assets
minus liabilities minus equity outside a 0.05 tolerance, or cash below the 25 minimum.

Setting FY2026 cash to the opening 40.4 instead of the computed 101.8 produces:

```
Pro-forma checks failed -- refusing to value these statements.
  FY2026E: balance sheet does not balance. Assets - liabilities - equity = -61.4
  FY2027E: balance sheet does not balance. Assets - liabilities - equity = -61.4
  ...
```

Exit code 1. No valuation is printed. The −61.4 is 40.4 − 101.8: the year's change in cash with
the sign flipped, which is the diagnostic — it names the size of the hole and the year it opened
before any cell is opened.

The gap repeats across all five years because the forced cash carries forward into each
subsequent opening balance. One broken year contaminates every year after it.

| Share buyback | judgment | raised: 150 → 400 | 277.80 | −4.8% |
| Tax rate | judgment | 25.5% → 26.5% | 287.06 | −1.6% |
| Other working capital | judgment | 0.8% → 1.8% | 289.40 | −0.8% |
| Share buyback | judgment | removed: 150 → 0 | 291.75 | 0.0% |
| Revolver rate | judgment | 6% → 7% | 291.75 | 0.0% |
| Revolver limit | judgment | 850 → 100 | 291.75 | 0.0% |

**My three.**

I chose terminal growth because my sensitivity analysis shows that a 1 percentage point change
in terminal growth causes a +13.2% change in value per share.

I chose gross margin because a 1 percentage point change in gross margin causes a +12.2% change
in value per share.

I chose SG&A because when you delete the assumed expansion altogether, it causes a −10.4% change
in value per share.

**Notes on the method.** Rows tested by removal or resizing are not comparable with the
one-percentage-point rows and are not ranked against them. Capital spending is included because
leaving it out would hide how much value it moves, but it is guidance rather than judgment — the
company supplied the figure, so it was never mine to choose.

Cost of equity at −13.0% is effectively tied with terminal growth. They are not two independent
judgments: both act on the `Ke − g` denominator, which is 7.5 points wide and carries roughly 80%
of the value. Terminal growth is the half with less external discipline — cost of equity has CAPM
behind it, while terminal growth is bounded only by long-run GDP.

**What changed.** My first answer, read off the assumption table before running anything, was
organic growth, the SG&A schedule, and the debt repayment / buyback pair. The sensitivity run
kept SG&A, demoted organic growth to fifth, and showed the buyback moving value by exactly zero.
Two of three replaced on the evidence.

## Floor plan

A floor plan is inventory loans from manufacturers' finance arms and banks. In this case, ABG
borrows against the cars on their lot so that they don't have to pay cash upfront. The floor plan
rises with inventory. There is interest on the opening balance and it sits in FCFE as operating,
not as financing.

**Why removing it sends cash to about −1.1 billion.** Setting the floor-plan-to-inventory ratio
to zero:

| Scenario | FY2026 FCFE | FY2026 cash | FY2027 FCFE |
|---|---|---|---|
| Base | 211.4 | 101.8 | 255.1 |
| No floor plan | **−1,852.4** | **−1,112.0** | 251.7 |

Strip the financing and ABG must fund its entire inventory book out of its own cash in year one.
FY2026 FCFE falls by roughly 2,064 — the 2,027 of financing that disappears, plus the 36.9 it
would have contributed. It is a one-time hit, not an ongoing drag: FY2027 onward returns to
near-normal at 251.7 against a base of 255.1, because there is no floor plan left to lose.

Two things the run shows that the headline number does not:

- **The revolver draws its full 850 every year and still cannot cover it.** The facility that
  moves value by 0.0% in the base case maxes out here and leaves the company $1.1bn short.
- **The balance sheet still balances perfectly** — gaps of 0.0 in all five years. The model
  refuses anyway, on the cash-minimum check. The arithmetic is internally consistent and the
  company is insolvent. Balancing and being viable are different tests, which is why
  `assert_balanced()` checks both.

ABG owns the cars. Inventory sits on the asset side at 2,135.8 because you do not book an asset
you do not own; floor plan is a loan secured by that inventory, not a consignment arrangement.

## Reflect

**Why cash is computed last.** Cash is computed last because it is a residual after earnings,
investments, borrowing, and paying.

The consequence is what makes the check worth running. If cash were plugged to force the sheet
to balance, the balance check could never fail, and a check that cannot fail proves nothing.

**What the −61.4 tells you before opening a cell.** The size of the hole and the year it opened.
Because it equals the year's change in cash with the sign reversed, it points at the cash line
specifically rather than anywhere else in the statements.

## Limitations

- **Buybacks do not retire shares.** The model spends 150 a year and holds the share count fixed
  at 17.951349 million. At roughly $292 a share that is close to 3% of the count annually, and
  the accretion is not captured. This is why the buyback tests at 0.0%.
- **The buyback is inert only while cash stays above the minimum.** Raised to 400 it moves value
  −4.8%, not because buybacks became valuable but because cash then falls through the floor, the
  revolver draws, and the interest feeds back into net income.
- **One-at-a-time sensitivity ignores interactions.** Moving growth and margin together is not
  the sum of moving each alone. The table ranks; it does not do scenario work.
- **Impairment is held flat at 120 a year**, which is a judgment with no filing behind it. Its
  only effect on cash is the tax it shields, so removing it makes the income statement look
  better and the company worth 7.2% less.
