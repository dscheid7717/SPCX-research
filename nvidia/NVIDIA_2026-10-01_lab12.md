## Stop 3 — My pro-forma

**History became assumptions through a labelled table** — every line carries `history`,
`guidance`, `judgment` or `fact`, and every judgment carries a reason.

| Ratio | FY2024 | FY2025 | FY2026 | My forecast |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| Gross margin | 72.72% | 74.99% | 71.07% | 71.0 → 69.0% (judgment) |
| R&D ÷ revenue | 14.24% | 9.90% | 8.57% | 8.5 → 10.5% (judgment) |
| SG&A ÷ gross profit | 5.99% | 3.57% | 2.98% | 3.0% flat (judgment) |
| Inventory days | 116.0 | 112.7 | 125.0 | 125.0 (history) |

**The line that reads backwards:** R&D and SG&A did not fall. In dollars both more than doubled.
They fell as *ratios* because revenue tripled underneath them — which is why I forecast R&D
*rising* as a ratio while growth decelerates.

**The company-specific driver: excess inventory purchase obligations**, 2,739 at FY2026 against
2,095 at FY2025, modelled at 12.80% of inventory. ABG's floor plan is a lender paying for the
cars on the lot. NVIDIA's analogue runs the other way: owning no fabs, it must commit to foundry
capacity years before it knows what it can sell, and the accrued loss on over-commitment lands
here. Mechanically identical to floor plan — scales with inventory, adds back to cash.
Economically opposite — for a dealer it signals the model working; here it signals over-ordering.

**Linked statements and checks.** Cash is computed **last**, never plugged.

| Check | Result |
|---|---|
| Opening sheet vs **filed** totals | 206,803 and 49,510, both exact |
| Balance, every forecast year | 0.0 |
| The refusal | `python proforma.py --prove-refusal` stops, exit 1 |

The refusal demo drops the stock-comp credit to equity; the gap it opens is **exactly** that
year's SBC. SBC must be booked twice — added back in cash flow because it moved no cash, *and*
credited to equity because it is paid in shares.

**A correction worth presenting.** My first version balanced perfectly and was still wrong: it
let surplus cash compound to 831,139 by FY2031 and earned interest on cash the valuation had
already treated as distributed. Holding the treasury flat moved value from **$62.43 to $58.23**.
The balance check could not catch it, because it was not a balancing error.

## Stop 4 — Valuation

| Method | Result | Date | Basis | Main limitation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **FCFF DCF** (Lab 06) | **$57.06**, grid $47.50–$72.36 | 2026-09-10 | enterprise, bridged to equity | WACC **16.01%** rests almost entirely on beta 2.217; TV is 59.3% of EV |
| **FCFE pro-forma** (Lab 10) | **$58.23** | 2026-09-24 | equity direct | same growth path and beta; TV is 59.0% of equity value |
| **Peer P/E** (Lab 08) | **$650.92**, range $370.66–$931.18 | 2026-09-10 | equity multiple | peers' GAAP earnings not economically comparable |
| *Market* | *$218.36 · $224.58* | *2026-09-10 · 09-24* | *observed* | *— not estimated* |

**The enterprise-to-equity bridge** (Lab 06): EV 1,344,683 **+** non-operating cash 62,556
**−** debt 8,468 **=** equity 1,398,771 **÷** 24,514 diluted shares **= $57.06**.
P/E is already an equity multiple, so no bridge applies there — that distinction is why the two
sit in different columns rather than being averaged.

**Why the DCF and the pro-forma agree** at $57.06 and $58.23 — 2.0% apart from different
statements. They share the growth path and beta deliberately, so this validates the *plumbing*,
not the inputs.

**Why the methods disagree by 11.4×, and why I am not averaging them.** An average of an estimate
I do not believe and another estimate I do not believe is not a third, better estimate. Each
fails in a way I can name:

- **The DCF fails on the discount rate.** NVIDIA is 99.8% equity-funded, so there is no debt
  weight to dilute beta — WACC inherits it entirely.
- **The comps fail on earnings comparability.** All three report total GAAP diluted EPS — the
  same *definition* — but not the same *thing*. AMD's is a thin residual after acquisition
  amortization equal to 61% of operating income; Broadcom's is half software; NVIDIA's carries
  $8.9B of non-cash investment gains.

**The one signal in the comps I do believe:** NVIDIA carries the **lowest** trailing GAAP P/E of
the three (44.6× vs AVGO 75.6× and AMD 190.0×) while earning the **highest** margin (55.6%) and
the **fastest** growth (+65.5%). That does not establish NVIDIA is cheap — but it does establish
it is not the expensive name in its own peer group, and my DCF gave me no way to see that.

**Reverse DCF (Lab 06).** Solving a uniform shift on all five FCFF growth rates to reach $218.36
gives **+42.88 percentage points** — a path of 72.88 / 64.88 / 58.88 / 53.88 / 49.88%.
**Held fixed:** starting FCFF 96,895.8, WACC 16.01%, terminal growth 3.00%, and the whole bridge.
**This is one assumption set consistent with the price. It is not proof of mispricing.**

**Reverse solves (Lab 10), same discipline:** the market price implies either a **6.54%** cost of
equity — a beta of **0.32**, against the 2.216 NVIDIA's own return history produces — or
**62.96%** revenue growth for five straight years, reaching $2.5 trillion of revenue.

## Stop 5 — Sensitivity and drivers

**Base:** FY2031E operating profit **255,618** · FY2031E FCFE **222,922** · **$58.23** per share.

| Driver, ±2pp all five years | Lower | Higher | Value span |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Revenue growth | 54.51 | 62.17 | **7.66** |
| Gross margin | 56.12 | 60.33 | **4.21** |

**Revenue growth is the larger driver, over these ranges** — 1.8× on value, 2.4× on operating
profit.

**Trace one result, input → statement → cash flow → value.** Gross margin higher, FY2031E
operating profit 264,772.17 against base 255,618.43, a difference of **+9,153.74** — and it
reduces to closed form, because SG&A is set as a ratio *to gross profit*:

```
0.02 x 471,842.44 x (1 - 0.03) = 9,153.74
```

From there: → net income at a 16% tax rate → **FCFE +7,873.58**, helped slightly because a higher
margin means lower cost of revenue, lower inventory and less cash tied up → **value +$2.11**,
amplified because the terminal value capitalises final-year FCFE and is 59.0% of the answer.

**What the ranking does NOT establish.** Both ranges are equal ±2pp **by design**, so the
comparison is between drivers rather than between my own generosity — but equal *points* are not
equal *economic* uncertainty. NVIDIA's growth swung 48.7 points year over year while margin swung
3.9 points in three years. Sized to real uncertainty, growth's lead would widen. **1.8× is a
floor, not a ceiling.** And none of it is a probability: the endpoints are values I chose, not
percentiles.

**Impact vs uncertainty, and the result that surprised me.** Gross margin's changes are *exactly*
symmetric (±9,153.74). Revenue growth's are not: **−$3.72 against +$3.94**. Growth is the only
input that is an argument of its own function, so it compounds — convex where margin is linear.
Colin's Microsoft model showed the same convexity, more pronounced, which is evidence it is a
property of compounding rather than a quirk of either model.

## Stop 6 — Interpretation

**The conditional recommendation I can support: WATCH–DEFER.**

**How my view changed.** Lab 06 said **do not initiate** on a $57.06 value against a $218.36
price. Lab 08 revised to **watch–defer** — on a *valuation-conviction* change, not a
business-quality one. Discovering that NVIDIA carries the lowest trailing multiple in its own
peer group while earning the best margins did not make my DCF wrong; it made me less willing to
act on it alone.

**What would change it.**

| To **initiate** | To **do not initiate** |
|---|---|
| Price below ~$114 (the 0.5× edge against my $57.06) | Compute & Networking growth decelerating faster than my 30% FY2027 path |
| A sourced, date-matched peer set showing AI silicon designers trade at 20–30× trailing GAAP earnings — which would make NVIDIA the outlier rather than my model | Gross margin falling below my 67% lower bound, signalling the competitive entry I assumed was gradual |
| A defensible case for a cost of equity materially below 16.03% | The supply-obligation ratio climbing above 12.8% of inventory — the first sign the build-out turned |

**The honest summary of the gap.** Both operating drivers together move value across
**$54.51–$62.17**. The market is at $224.58. **No operating assumption I can defend from NVIDIA's
own history closes a $166 gap** — so the disagreement is not about operations. It is about the
discount rate, or about growth far outside any range my evidence supports.

**The evidence I would investigate next: beta.** It is the single input carrying the answer, it
is explicitly *out of scope* for Lab 11 because it is a valuation input rather than an operating
driver, and my own estimate ranges 1.815 to 2.216 across defensible specifications — a 201 basis
point swing in the discount rate. Second priority: Compute & Networking segment growth, which is
effectively the whole story at 89.6% of revenue.
