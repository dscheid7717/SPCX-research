**The line that reads backwards:** R&D and SG&A did not fall. In dollars both more than doubled.
They fell as *ratios* because revenue tripled underneath them — which is why I forecast R&D
*rising* as a ratio while growth decelerates.

**The company-specific driver: excess inventory purchase obligations**, 2,739 at FY2026 against
2,095 at FY2025, modelled at 12.80% of inventory. ABG's floor plan is a lender paying for the
cars on the lot. NVIDIA's analogue runs the other way: owning no fabs, it must commit to foundry
capacity years before it knows what it can sell, and the accrued loss on over-commitment lands
here. Mechanically identical to floor plan — scales with inventory, adds back to cash.
Economically opposite — for a dealer it signals the model working; here it signals over-ordering.

**The enterprise-to-equity bridge** (Lab 06): EV 1,344,683 **+** non-operating cash 62,556

**Why the DCF and the pro-forma agree** at $57.06 and $58.23 — 2.0% apart from different
statements. They share the growth path and beta deliberately, so this validates the *plumbing*,

**Why the methods disagree by 11.4×, and why I am not averaging them.** An average of an estimate
I do not believe and another estimate I do not believe is not a third, better estimate. Each
fails in a way I can name:

- **The DCF fails on the discount rate.** NVIDIA is 99.8% equity-funded, so there is no debt

- **The comps fail on earnings comparability.** All three report total GAAP diluted EPS — the
  same *definition* — but not the same *thing*. AMD's is a thin residual after acquisition
  amortization equal to 61% of operating income; Broadcom's is half software; NVIDIA's carries
  $8.9B of non-cash investment gains.

**The one signal in the comps I do believe:** NVIDIA carries the **lowest** trailing GAAP P/E of
the three (44.6× vs AVGO 75.6× and AMD 190.0×) while earning the **highest** margin (55.6%) and
the **fastest** growth (+65.5%). That does not establish NVIDIA is cheap — but it does establish
it is not the expensive name in its own peer group, and my DCF gave me no way to see that.

**Reverse DCF (Lab 06).** Solving a uniform shift on all five FCFF growth rates to reach $218.36

**This is one assumption set consistent with the price. It is not proof of mispricing.**

### Correction I owe Colin — how the beta was actually built

**What I told him:** that I calculated beta from the financial statements and peers, then checked
it against the posted beta.

**What [`beta.py`](beta.py) actually does:** it estimates beta as the **ordinary least squares
slope of NVDA's returns regressed on the market's**, using five years of monthly adjusted closes
against the **S&P 500 (`^GSPC`)**. The inputs are **price returns only**. There are no financial
statements in it and no peer betas — no bottom-up or unlevered-relevered build of any kind.

Only the last clause of my answer was right. The regression returned **2.216**, and the Lab 06
provider beta carried in `dcf.py` is **2.217**, so my own estimate did confirm the posted figure
almost exactly — which is why the mistake was easy to make and why it did not change any number.

The fuller answer, which is the one in the file:

- Beta is not a fact to look up; it is **the slope of a regression**, and the slope moves with
  three analyst choices — how far back, how often to sample, and which index is "the market."
- So the file reports **five specifications**, not one: 5-year monthly, 2-year weekly, 5-year
  weekly, 1-year daily, 2-year daily, each with a standard error.
- **5-year monthly is the base case**, chosen *before* seeing results because a perpetuity is a
  long-horizon claim.
- The same figure is produced twice by independent routes — the regression slope, and
  correlation × (σ_stock ÷ σ_market) — and they agree to 1e-12 on live data.
- The Blume adjustment shrinks it a third of the way toward 1.0, giving **1.815** and a 14.02%
  cost of equity against 16.03%. That 201-basis-point swing is why I report a range.

**Why this matters more than a slip.** Beta is the input I named at Stop 6 as the one carrying
the whole answer, and it is the thing I must explain on camera. Misdescribing its method is the
single worst place to be loose. Recorded here rather than quietly fixed, because the lab asks for

## Reflect

Not because the answer changed — the regression returns 2.216 and the provider figure is 2.217,
so no number moved. It made me reconsider because I answered it **wrong about my own model**. I

says that in its first paragraph and reports five specifications for exactly that reason. The
spread from 1.815 to 2.216 is a 201 basis point swing in the discount rate, and at Stop 6 I named
it as the single input carrying the answer. Being unable to describe how I built it is the
clearest signal I have that it is where the next work goes.

The second thing: a review can be useful without changing a conclusion. Nothing Colin asked
produced new evidence about NVIDIA, and the call did not move. What moved was my ability to
defend it.
