### How a peer's P/E becomes a price for NVIDIA

Stated plainly, because it is the whole method and everything below is an argument about whether
its inputs are sound:

1. **The peer supplies a multiple.** Broadcom's price divided by Broadcom's annual diluted EPS —
   $360.83 ÷ $4.77 = **75.645702×** — is what investors currently pay for one dollar of
   Broadcom's annual earnings.
2. **The target supplies the earnings.** NVIDIA earned **$4.90** per diluted share in FY2026.
3. **Multiply.** 75.645702 × $4.90 = **$370.66**. That is what one NVIDIA share would be worth
   *if* investors priced NVIDIA's dollar of earnings exactly the way they price Broadcom's.

The peer never supplies the earnings and the target never supplies the multiple. The whole
comparison rests on step 3's "if" — which is the assumption this lab spends its length testing.

Because P/E is an **equity** multiple, $370.66 is already a value per share to shareholders. No
cash is added and no debt subtracted; that bridge belongs to the enterprise-value DCF in Lab 06.

## Define/Discover — understanding NVIDIA before choosing anyone to compare it to

> **The question:** what would one NVIDIA share be worth at defensible peer P/E multiples, and
> how does that compare with my Lab 06 discounted cash flow?

**How NVIDIA earns money.** It designs accelerated-computing silicon and sells it, largely as
full data-center systems, to cloud providers and enterprises building AI infrastructure. It owns
no fabs. FY2026 revenue was **$215,938**, of which **Compute & Networking was $193,479 (89.6%)**
— this is effectively a single growth story, not a diversified portfolio (`TABLE5`).

**What that implies for the peer search.** The economics to match are: a fabless designer whose
silicon is bought for data-center AI workloads, manufactured by third-party foundries, sold
through OEM/ODM channels into a concentrated set of hyperscale customers. Sharing the label
"semiconductor company" is not enough — a memory maker, an analog supplier and a foundry all
carry that label and none of them earns money the way NVIDIA does.

**Are NVIDIA's annual earnings positive?** Yes. FY2026 (ended January 25, 2026) reported
**total GAAP diluted EPS of $4.90** on net income of $120,067 (`INCOME_STATEMENT` rows 30 and
34). The 10-K was filed February 25, 2026, well before the September 10, 2026 comparison date.
A P/E is arithmetically available. Whether it is *meaningful* is the question this lab answers.

**What I still needed to research:** each candidate's actual segment mix and manufacturing
model from its own Item 1, its fiscal-year end date, and — the item I suspected would matter
most — what non-operating and acquisition-related items sit inside each company's GAAP earnings.

## Represent — the peer policy, written before any candidate was named

1. **Business model** — a publicly listed **fabless** semiconductor designer whose wafers are
   produced by third-party foundries, selling compute or networking silicon into **data-center
   AI infrastructure** as a material part of revenue.
2. **Economics** — comparable exposure to hyperscaler capital spending, comparable reliance on
   external foundry capacity, and a revenue base that is predominantly silicon rather than
   software, services or manufacturing-for-hire.
3. **Earnings** — **positive total GAAP diluted EPS** for a full fiscal year, from an annual
   report published **before September 10, 2026**. Reported GAAP only. Not non-GAAP, not
   management's adjusted figure, not a consensus forward estimate, not a single quarter.
4. **Disposition** — **use** a candidate that fits cleanly; **qualify** one carrying a material
   but disclosed and verifiable difference; **exclude** one that fails the business-model or
   earnings test.

### Evidence that would make me reject a candidate — written before the searches returned

- Owns and operates its own wafer fabs, or sells foundry capacity to others (a different
  capital-intensity and margin structure entirely).
- Negative or zero annual GAAP diluted EPS.
- No data-center AI silicon revenue of consequence — a memory, analog, or handset-modem
  business wearing the same industry label.
- Latest annual report published *after* September 10, 2026, or covering a stub period.
- Business-model claim I can only source to an AI assertion rather than to the company's own
  Item 1.

**The policy was not revised after seeing the numbers.** What the numbers changed was the
*weight* I put on the result, not the admission rules — that distinction is the whole substance
of the Evolve section below.

## The two candidate decisions

### Advanced Micro Devices (AMD) — **Qualify**

**Business model — matches.** AMD's Item 1 states its three reportable segments are "the **Data
Center segment**, which primarily includes AI accelerators, CPUs for servers, GPUs, APUs, DPUs,
AI NICs, FPGAs and SoC products for data centers," plus Client and Gaming, and Embedded. Item 1,
*Manufacturing Arrangements — Third-Party Wafer Foundry Facilities*: "We utilize **Taiwan
Semiconductor Manufacturing Company Limited (TSMC)** for the production of wafers for our HPC,
FPGA and adaptive SoC products." Fabless, data-center AI silicon, same foundry. Of any listed
company this is the closest match to NVIDIA's business.

**Source:** [AMD Form 10-K, FY ended December 27, 2025, filed February 4, 2026](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2488/000000248826000018/amd-20251227.htm)
— Item 1, *Business* (segment description and Manufacturing Arrangements); Note 4, *Segment
Reporting*.

**The material difference — earnings composition, not business model.** AMD's own Note 4 splits
FY2025 this way:

| AMD FY2025 | Revenue | Operating income |
|---|---:|---:|
| Data Center | 16,635 | 3,603 |
| Client and Gaming | 14,550 | 2,855 |
| Embedded | 3,454 | 1,243 |
| *Segment total* | *34,639* | *7,701* |
| **All Other** | — | **(4,007)** |
| **GAAP operating income** | **34,639** | **3,694** |

All Other is **52.0% of segment operating income**, and AMD itself discloses its contents:
**amortization of acquisition-related intangibles $2,254**, stock-based compensation $1,638,
acquisition-related and other costs $228, net of small credits. The largest single item is a
non-cash charge arising from past acquisitions.

The consequence for this comparison: **$2,254 of acquisition-intangible amortization equals
61.0% of AMD's entire GAAP operating income of $3,694.** NVIDIA's *total* depreciation and
amortization for FY2026 — everything, not just acquisition intangibles — was **$2,843 against
operating income of $130,387, or 2.2%** (`CASH_FLOW` row 20, `INCOME_STATEMENT` row 23).

Both companies report on the same GAAP *definition*. They are not reporting on the same
*economics*. AMD's denominator is a small residual after a large non-cash acquisition charge;
NVIDIA's is not. **AMD is retained as a qualified peer** — the business model genuinely matches
and the difference is disclosed and quantifiable, which is exactly what a qualification is for.

### Broadcom (AVGO) — **Qualify**

**Business model — partially matches.** Item 1, *Overview*: "We are a global technology leader
that designs, develops and supplies a broad range of **semiconductor and semiconductor-based
solutions and infrastructure software solutions**." Its semiconductor solutions are used in
"enterprise and artificial intelligence ('AI') data centers, servers and networking and
connectivity equipment." Broadcom designs custom AI accelerators (XPUs) and Ethernet networking
silicon for hyperscalers — in data-center AI networking it is NVIDIA's most direct listed
competitor.

**Source:** [Broadcom Form 10-K, FY ended November 2, 2025, filed December 18, 2025](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1730168/000173016825000121/avgo-20251102.htm)
— Item 1, *Business* (Overview and reportable segments); Note 13, *Segment Information*.

**Two material differences, both quantified from Note 13.**

**1. Nearly half the company is not a semiconductor business.**

| Broadcom FY2025 | Revenue | Share | Operating income | Share |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Semiconductor solutions | 36,858 | 57.7% | 21,232 | 50.6% |
| **Infrastructure software** | **27,029** | **42.3%** | **20,765** | **49.4%** |
| *Segment total* | *63,887* | | *41,997* | |
| Unallocated (amortization 8,062, SBC 7,568, restructuring 667, deal costs 216) | | | (16,513) | |
| **GAAP operating income** | | | **25,484** | |

Infrastructure software — private cloud, mainframe, cybersecurity, enterprise software, largely
VMware — is **42.3% of revenue and 49.4% of segment operating income**. Roughly half of the EPS
in the denominator of Broadcom's multiple is earned by a software business with different
margins, different growth and different risk from anything NVIDIA does.

**2. The same acquisition-amortization distortion, at larger absolute scale.** Amortization of
acquisition-related intangibles was **$8,062**, or **31.6% of GAAP operating income**, against
NVIDIA's 2.2% all-in D&A ratio.

**Broadcom is retained as a qualified peer.** It is the best available listed comparison for
data-center AI silicon, and both differences are disclosed, sourced and measurable rather than
hidden inside an average.

### Intel (INTC) — **Exclude**

Screened out on the business-model test first: Intel owns and operates its own wafer fabs and
runs a foundry business selling capacity to others, which is the opposite of the fabless model
the policy requires. It **also** fails the earnings test — FY2025 (ended December 27, 2025)
total GAAP diluted EPS was **$(0.06)**, filed January 23, 2026. A negative denominator produces
a negative number that looks like a multiple and means nothing.

**Source:** [Intel Form 10-K, FY ended December 27, 2025, filed January 23, 2026](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/50863/000005086326000011/intc-20251227.htm),
accession 0000050863-26-000011; EPS verified against
[SEC XBRL `EarningsPerShareDiluted`](https://data.sec.gov/api/xbrl/companyconcept/CIK0000050863/us-gaap/EarningsPerShareDiluted.json).

The exclusion is retained here rather than deleted, and `comps.py` prints it on every run, so
the screen-out stays visible. Note the order: it was excluded on **business model**, before the
EPS was looked at. Letting the arithmetic make the business decision would have been the wrong
way round.

Peer EPS figures were taken from the SEC's XBRL `EarningsPerShareDiluted` concept, which returns

**Currency basis.** All four companies report in **USD** and all four are quoted in USD on
Nasdaq, so no exchange-rate conversion enters any multiple. Had a peer reported in another
currency, its price and EPS would have to be converted at a consistent rate and date before the
division — a mismatch there is the same class of error as a stock-split mismatch.

### Share-basis check — the control against a split mismatch

A P/E is wrong by a factor of ten if the price is on one stock-split basis and the EPS on
another. The calculator tests this by recovering each company's own diluted share count from its
own filing:

| Ticker | Implied diluted shares (net income ÷ EPS) | × price = implied market cap |
|---|---:|---:|
| NVDA | 24,503M | $5,350,578M |
| AMD | 1,636M | $823,814M |
| AVGO | 4,848M | $1,749,383M |

Each implied market capitalisation is the size the company actually is. A price/EPS pair on
mismatched split bases would be out by the split factor and would be obvious here. NVDA's
implied 24,503M against the filing's stated 24,514M differs by 0.05%, entirely from the filing
rounding $4.8979 to $4.90 — which also confirms the check is sensitive enough to be worth
running.

### Earnings-basis test — what NVIDIA's own EPS carries

P/E multiplies the *target's* EPS, so anything unusual in that EPS flows into every implied
price. NVIDIA's FY2026 net income includes **$8,918 of gains on non-marketable and publicly-held
equity securities** (`CASH_FLOW` rows 21–22) — the same figure the cash flow statement removes
as a **non-cash** reconciling item. Against a prior-year $1,030, that is a $7,888 swing in
investment gains, not in chip sales.

| Target EPS basis | EPS | Implied at peer median |
|---|---:|---:|
| Reported diluted EPS | $4.90 | $650.92 |
| Computed (net income ÷ diluted shares) | $4.90 | $650.64 |
| **Excluding equity-securities gains** | **$4.59** | **$609.62** |

Removing the gains at NVIDIA's own **15.1170%** effective rate (21,383 ÷ 141,450) lowers EPS by
**6.3%** and every implied price with it. The peer multiples are identical across all three
rows, so the entire difference is the target's earnings basis.

**This is a sensitivity, not a restatement.** The blended effective rate is an approximation —
investment gains need not be taxed at the company's average rate — and I have not substituted an
adjusted EPS anywhere in the headline comparison. The point is that **$0.31 of NVIDIA's $4.90,
and $41.30 of the $650.92 implied price, comes from marking investments rather than from
operating the business.**

## Reflect — the conditional call

### **WATCH–DEFER.** Revised from Lab 06's *do not initiate*.

**What changed and what did not.** The *action* is unchanged: do not buy. The *reason and the
conviction* change. Lab 06 said do-not-initiate because a DCF put fair value at $57.06 against a
$218.36 price. This lab's external evidence shows that DCF is out of step with how the market
prices every comparable company I could source, so I am no longer willing to present $57.06 as a
fair value. Downgrading the confidence while holding the action is the honest revision; holding
a strong negative call on a model this lab has undermined would not be.

**I withhold a defensible valuation range, and I can say precisely why.**

- The DCF range ($47.50–$72.36) rests on a WACC that trips the course's own re-check threshold
  and is contradicted by the market's pricing of the whole sector.
- The peer range ($370.66–$931.18) rests on two peers whose GAAP earnings are not economically
  comparable to NVIDIA's, and 43.1% of it rests on either one alone.

Two ranges that disagree by eleven-fold do not average into a third. **The defensible output of
this lab is a diagnosis, not a price.**

**What the comparison adds to the DCF.** It supplies the external check the DCF structurally
cannot: evidence sourced from prices other investors are actually paying, rather than from my own
forecast. Its specific contribution is not the implied range — that is unusable — but the
relative reading that NVIDIA trades at the lowest trailing multiple of its group while earning
the best margin and the fastest growth. That directly contradicts my DCF, and I would rather
hold a contradiction I can name than an answer I cannot defend.

**Why the two results differ.** A DCF values the company on *my* forecast of its cash flows; a
multiple values it on *the market's* expectations for its peers. They diverge here because both
are being asked to price a company mid-buildout using backward-looking inputs — trailing GAAP
