## FCFF build

The lab defines FCFF as **operating cash flow + after-tax interest paid − capital expenditure**.

| Component | Value | Locator |
|---|---:|---|
| Net cash provided by operating activities | 102,718 | `CASH_FLOW` row 32 |
| Interest expense | 259 | `INCOME_STATEMENT` row 24 |
| Effective tax rate | 15.12% | 21,383 ÷ 141,450 (`INCOME_STATEMENT` rows 27–28) |
| After-tax interest | +219.8 | 259 × (1 − 0.1512) |
| Purchases related to property, equipment and intangible assets | (6,042) | `CASH_FLOW` rows 40–41 |
| **Starting FCFF** | **96,895.8** | |

**Two judgment calls, both stated rather than buried:**

1. The 10-K does **not** disclose *cash paid for interest* — the supplemental disclosures
   (`CASH_FLOW` rows 58–59) show only cash paid for income taxes. Accrued **interest expense** of
   $259M is used as the proxy. At 0.2% of FCFF this cannot change the answer.
2. Capex uses the $6,042M investing-activities line. Finance-lease principal of $101M
   (`CASH_FLOW` rows 50–51, in financing) is excluded. Also excluded: the $13,000M **Groq, Inc.**
   purchase and $1,535M of other acquisitions — these are acquisitions, not maintenance or
   growth capex on existing operations.

### The working-capital distortion — worth knowing before you defend this number

FY2026 net income was $120,067M but operating cash flow was $102,718M. The gap is a
**$15,949M net working-capital build** — accounts receivable −15,399 and inventories −11,324,
partly offset by payables and accruals (`CASH_FLOW` rows 26–31).

That drag is what hypergrowth costs in cash. If growth decelerates, it reverses and becomes a
cash *source*. Excluding it entirely gives an FCFF of $112,845M — about 16% higher — and lifts
value per share only from $57.06 to $66.09 (0.26x to 0.30x of market price) at the WACC and
growth path below. **It does not close the gap to the market price**, so it is a caveat rather
than an explanation.

## WACC build — `estimate`

Per the lab's formula: cost of equity ≈ risk-free rate + beta × 5%; cost of debt ≈ debt-note
rate × (1 − tax rate); weight by market value.

| Component | Value | Source |
|---|---:|---|
| Risk-free rate | 4.944% | 10-year US Treasury (`^TNX`), close 2026-09-10 |
| Beta | 2.217 | Yahoo Finance published beta, retrieved 2026-09-10 |
| Equity risk premium | 5.00% | Lab-specified convention |
| **Cost of equity** | **16.03%** | 4.944% + 2.217 × 5% |
| Pre-tax cost of debt | 2.92% | Principal-weighted effective rates, `TABLE32` rows 19–25 |
| **After-tax cost of debt** | **2.48%** | 2.92% × (1 − 0.1512) |
| Market value of equity | 5,352,877 | $218.36 × 24,514M shares |
| Market value of debt | 8,468 | Note 11 net carrying amount (book used as proxy) |
| Debt weight | **0.158%** | 8,468 ÷ 5,361,345 |
| **WACC** | **16.01%** | |

**Nvidia is effectively unlevered.** Debt is one-sixth of one percent of total capital, so the
WACC is its cost of equity to two decimal places. The entire discount rate rests on beta.

## Growth, Years 1–5 — `forecast`

| Year 1 | Year 2 | Year 3 | Year 4 | Year 5 |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 30% | 22% | 16% | 11% | 7% |

**This is a forecast, not a sourced figure.** These are **FCFF** growth rates, not revenue growth
rates — the two differ because working capital and capex sit between revenue and free cash flow.

### What it is anchored to

**Reported FCFF history**, computed on the same OCF + after-tax interest − capex definition the

|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|

| FY2026 (ended 2026-01-25) | 102,718 | 6,042 | 15.12% | 96,895.8 | **+58.7%** | +65.5% |

**Segment composition** (`TABLE5`, revenue by reportable segment, FY2026):

| Segment | Revenue | Change |
|---|---:|---:|
| Compute & Networking | 193,479 | +67% |
| Graphics | 22,459 | +57% |
| **Total** | **215,938** | **+65%** |

Compute & Networking is 89.6% of revenue — this is effectively a single growth story.

### Reasoning

The path continues the observed deceleration (+124.1% → +58.7% → 30% → fading) rather than
assuming either a cliff or a plateau. Year 5 at 7% is close enough to the 3% terminal rate that
the Gordon fade is not a discontinuity, which matters because 59.3% of enterprise value sits in
the terminal value.

Note that FCFF grew *slower* than revenue in FY2026 (+58.7% vs +65.5%) as working capital
absorbed cash, and capex roughly doubled in each of the last two years. Both argue against
assuming FCFF simply tracks revenue growth.

### The conclusion does not depend on this choice

Four alternative paths, all at WACC 16.01% and terminal growth 3%:

| Path | Years 1–5 | Value/share | vs price |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| Sharp deceleration | 20/14/10/7/5% | $45.99 | 0.21x |
| **Conservative fade (selected)** | **30/22/16/11/7%** | **$57.06** | **0.26x** |
| Trend-following fade | 45/35/25/16/10% | $77.66 | 0.36x |
| Aggressive | 55/45/35/25/15% | $103.21 | 0.47x |

Every path lands outside the 0.5x–2.0x reasonableness band. The growth forecast changes the
magnitude of the gap to market price, not the direction of the conclusion.

### Reverse DCF

**Solved variable:** a uniform shift added to all five explicit FCFF growth rates.
**Target price:** $218.36. **Solved shift: +42.88 percentage points.**

| Year | Forecast path | Path the price implies |
|---|---:|---:|
| 1 | 30.00% | 72.88% |
| 2 | 22.00% | 64.88% |
| 3 | 16.00% | 58.88% |
| 4 | 11.00% | 53.88% |
| 5 | 7.00% | 49.88% |

**Held fixed while solving:** starting FCFF ($96,895.8M); WACC (16.01%); terminal growth (3.00%);
and the bridge — non-operating cash, debt, and diluted shares. Solved by bisection between −20
and +150 percentage points; the search refuses any bracket that would drive an annual growth
rate to −100% or below, and reports no solution rather than returning a bound.

**This is one set of assumptions consistent with the price. It is not proof of mispricing.**

## Conditional call

**Do not initiate.** At a share price of $218.36, my DCF supports no margin of safety under any
assumption set I tested. **Initiate if** the price falls below **$114** — the 0.5x edge of the
reasonableness band against my $57.06 value — **otherwise remain out.**

**Monitor:** Compute & Networking segment revenue growth. This segment makes up almost 90% of
revenue and is effectively the entire growth story.

**The input I distrust most is the 16.01% WACC.** It rests almost entirely on NVIDIA's published
beta of 2.217, a measure of the stock's volatility against the market. Because NVIDIA is roughly
99.8% equity-funded, there is no meaningful debt weight to dilute that beta, so the WACC is
essentially the cost of equity and the whole discount rate turns on one input. Notably, that beta
is high *despite* the absence of leverage, which means it reflects business risk rather than
financial risk.

**On the limits of this model:** a four-fold gap between my value and a $5 trillion market
capitalization more likely indicates that a five-year explicit forecast with a hard 3% terminal
fade is too narrow a frame for a company mid-buildout than that the market is wrong by 4x. The
model's own diagnostic supports that caution — 59.3% of enterprise value sits in the terminal
value. The reverse DCF above states what the price assumes; it does not establish mispricing.
