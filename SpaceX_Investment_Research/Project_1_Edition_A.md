# Project 1 — Edition A: SpaceX Investment Analysis

## Decision and User

**Decision:** Whether the fund should initiate a position in Space Exploration Technologies Corp. (NASDAQ: SPCX).

**User:** The fund's investment committee, which needs a risk-aware preliminary recommendation and a focused diligence plan before committing capital.

## Company and Valuation Date

**Company:** Space Exploration Technologies Corp. (SpaceX), including its Space, Connectivity, and AI reportable segments.

**Valuation date:** September 1, 2026. Edition A does not assert fair value because the current share price, fully diluted share count, and normalized forward financials have not yet been verified.

## Initial Thesis

**WATCH–DEFER.** Do not initiate a position until valuation and the durability of consolidated free cash flow can be tested.

The positive case is a rapidly growing and profitable Connectivity business, led by Starlink, supported by substantial liquidity and differentiated launch infrastructure. The offset is that AI and Space losses, exceptional capital spending, increased debt, declining Starlink ARPU, and volatile below-the-line expenses make consolidated earnings and free cash flow difficult to underwrite. The operating assets may be exceptional, but the available evidence does not yet show that the stock offers an adequate risk-adjusted return at the September 1, 2026 market price.

## Known Evidence

All dollar amounts are in millions unless stated otherwise.

- Q2 2026 revenue was **$7,814**, up **91.9%** year over year. Six-month revenue was **$12,508**, up **53.7%**.
- Q2 net loss improved to **$(541)** from **$(1,008)**. The six-month net loss worsened to **$(4,817)** from **$(1,536)**, reflecting higher R&D, interest expense, other expense, and a debt-extinguishment loss.
- Adjusted EBITDA was **$3,538** in Q2 and **$4,665** for the first six months, versus $1,214 and $2,944 in the comparable 2025 periods. Adjusted EBITDA adds back substantial depreciation, share-based compensation, interest, and other items, so it is not a substitute for free cash flow.
- Connectivity generated six-month revenue of **$7,548** and operating income of **$2,844**. AI generated revenue of **$3,379** but an operating loss of **$(3,726)**. Space generated revenue of **$1,581** and an operating loss of **$(1,204)**.
- Starlink subscribers doubled to approximately **12 million** from 6 million, while monthly ARPU declined to **$66** from $85.
- Six-month operating cash flow was **$3,466**, while purchases of property, plant, and equipment were **$(28,476)**. On a simple operating-cash-flow-minus-capex basis, free cash flow was approximately **$(25,010)**.
- AI accounted for **$23,551**, or approximately **82.7%**, of first-half capital expenditures.
- The IPO provided **$85,675** of net proceeds. Cash, cash equivalents, and restricted cash ended June at **$94,352**.
- Debt and finance leases totaled **$39,364**, up from **$22,896** at December 31, 2025. First-half interest expense was **$1,293**.
- Customer concentration is meaningful: Customer A represented 17.9% and Customer B 12.2% of six-month revenue.
- Falcon launches declined to 77 from 81 and total mass to orbit declined to 1,041 metric tons from 1,102 in the comparable six-month period.

## Consequential Assumptions

- Starlink subscriber growth can offset declining ARPU without materially weakening service quality or unit economics.
- Connectivity operating profit is sustainable and can fund part of the investment required by Space and AI.
- AI capital spending will eventually produce returns above the fund's cost of capital; current revenue growth alone does not establish this.
- First-half 2026 capital spending is elevated but not a permanent run rate. If it persists, external financing or a prolonged cash draw becomes more likely.
- Adjusted EBITDA is directionally informative, but valuation must be anchored to normalized free cash flow because depreciation, share-based compensation, interest, and capital intensity are economically consequential.
- The post-merger segment reporting and capital allocation are sufficiently transparent to value Space, Connectivity, and AI separately.
- Any initiation requires a discount to conservative sum-of-the-parts value and adequate downside protection under slower subscriber growth, continued ARPU pressure, and sustained AI losses.

## Unknowns

- September 1 share price, enterprise value, fully diluted share count, and the dilution effect of outstanding equity awards.
- Sustainable Starlink churn, subscriber acquisition cost, contribution margin, capacity utilization, and pricing by geography and customer type.
- The normalized level and duration of AI infrastructure capital expenditures and when, if ever, AI becomes free-cash-flow positive.
- Segment-level free cash flow, intercompany arrangements, and capital allocation priorities.
- Revenue backlog, launch contract economics, Starship development milestones, and expected returns on launch infrastructure.
- The composition of the **$(1,962)** first-half other expense and whether similar volatility should be expected.
- Debt maturities, refinancing costs, covenants, and the expected use of the unusually large IPO cash balance.
- Regulatory, spectrum, launch-failure, satellite-replacement, cybersecurity, geopolitical, key-person, and governance risks.
- Appropriate public-company peers and valuation multiples for each segment.

## Research Plan

1. **Establish valuation:** Verify the September 1 share price, diluted shares, cash, debt, and enterprise value. Reconcile post-IPO and merger-related securities.
2. **Build a segment model:** Forecast subscribers, ARPU, Connectivity margins, launch cadence, Space economics, AI revenue, and segment operating costs through at least 2030.
3. **Reconstruct free cash flow:** Separate maintenance from growth capex, normalize working capital and share-based compensation, and build base, upside, and downside cases.
4. **Value the parts:** Use a DCF and relevant public-company multiples for Connectivity, Space, and AI; subtract net debt and apply a conglomerate/governance discount where justified.
5. **Stress the thesis:** Test slower subscriber growth, further ARPU compression, launch disruption, persistent AI losses, higher funding costs, and sustained first-half capex intensity.
6. **Investigate the unknowns:** Review the full 10-Q narrative, S-1, merger filings, debt footnotes, earnings materials, regulatory filings, and credible industry data. Prioritize primary sources.
7. **Set decision thresholds:** Recommend initiation only if conservative fair value offers an adequate margin of safety and downside liquidity remains acceptable without repeated external financing.

## Edition A Conclusion

SpaceX merits continued diligence, but the current evidence supports **watch–defer**, not initiation. The next decision should be driven by price-to-conservative-value and a credible path from segment operating performance to consolidated free cash flow.
