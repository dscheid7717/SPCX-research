# SpaceX Company Research Report

**Company:** Space Exploration Technologies Corp. (SpaceX)[^15]  
**Ticker:** SPCX[^15]  
**Report date:** September 3, 2026 — **UNSOURCED — analyst report date.**  
**Financial period reviewed:** Quarter and six months ended June 30, 2026.[^15]  
**Initial call:** **WATCH–DEFER — UNSOURCED — analyst judgment.**

SpaceX is a publicly traded technology and infrastructure company with three reportable segments: Space, Connectivity, and AI.[^1] The company completed its initial public offering in June 2026, selling 638.9 million Class A shares at $135 per share and receiving $85.675 billion of net proceeds.[^2]

## Investment View

My call is **watch–defer**: the fund should not initiate a position until valuation and the durability of consolidated free cash flow can be tested. **UNSOURCED — analyst judgment.**

The positive case is a rapidly growing and profitable Connectivity business, led by Starlink, supported by substantial liquidity and differentiated launch infrastructure. The offset is that AI and Space losses, exceptional capital spending, increased debt, declining Starlink average revenue per user (ARPU), and volatile below-the-line expenses make consolidated earnings and free cash flow difficult to underwrite. The operating assets may be exceptional, but the current work does not yet demonstrate an adequate risk-adjusted return. **UNSOURCED — analyst synthesis and judgment based on the sourced evidence below.**

## Company Overview

SpaceX describes itself as a designer, manufacturer, launcher, and operator of rockets, spacecraft, communications networks, and AI products and infrastructure.[^1] Its Space segment develops and launches reusable rockets; its Connectivity segment operates Starlink; and its AI segment includes Grok, X, AI solutions, and computational infrastructure.[^1]

At June 30, 2026, Starlink operated more than 10,200 broadband and mobile satellites and served customers across 167 countries, territories, and other markets.[^1] Reported Starlink subscribers reached approximately 12 million, compared with 6 million a year earlier, while monthly ARPU declined to $66 from $85.[^3]

## Financial Performance

All dollar amounts in this report are in millions unless stated otherwise.[^4]

Second-quarter revenue was $7,814, up 91.9% from $4,071 in the prior-year quarter.[^4] Revenue for the first six months was $12,508, up 53.7% from $8,138.[^4]

The second-quarter net loss improved to $(541) from $(1,008), but the six-month net loss widened to $(4,817) from $(1,536).[^4] First-half research and development expense rose to $7,062 from $3,515, interest expense rose to $1,293 from $858, and other expense was $(1,962) compared with other income of $202.[^4] The filing also reports a $1,545 loss on debt extinguishment in the first six months of 2026.[^5]

Adjusted EBITDA was $3,538 in the second quarter and $4,665 for the first six months, compared with $1,214 and $2,944 in the respective 2025 periods.[^6] The company's reconciliation adds back depreciation and amortization, share-based compensation, restructuring, impairments, interest, other expense, and income taxes.[^6] My view is that adjusted EBITDA is directionally informative but is not a substitute for free cash flow because several of those add-backs are economically consequential. **UNSOURCED — analyst judgment.**

## Segment Review

### Connectivity

Connectivity was the largest and most profitable segment in the first half of 2026, reporting $7,548 of revenue and $2,844 of operating income.[^7] Revenue increased from $5,062 and operating income increased from $1,956 in the first half of 2025.[^7] The segment's reported revenue consisted primarily of Consumer and Enterprise & Government connectivity revenue.[^8]

Connectivity is the strongest support for the investment case because it combines high growth with positive operating income. **UNSOURCED — analyst judgment.** The decline in Starlink ARPU from $85 to $66 remains important because subscriber growth must continue to offset lower revenue per user. **UNSOURCED — analyst interpretation based on reported subscriber and ARPU data.**

### AI

AI produced $3,379 of first-half revenue, up from $1,465, but generated a $(3,726) operating loss compared with $(2,460) in the prior-year period.[^7] The segment recorded $23,551 of first-half capital expenditures, compared with $3,316 a year earlier.[^7] AI therefore represented approximately 82.7% of consolidated first-half capital expenditures, calculated from the segment's $23,551 and the company's $28,476 total.[^5][^7]

AI is the central underwriting risk because its revenue growth has not yet translated into operating profit and its infrastructure requires substantial capital. **UNSOURCED — analyst judgment.**

### Space

Space generated $1,581 of first-half revenue, compared with $1,611 in 2025, and its operating loss widened to $(1,204) from $(439).[^7] Falcon launches decreased to 77 from 81, and total mass delivered to orbit decreased to 1,041 metric tons from 1,102 in the comparable six-month period.[^9]

The Space segment supplies strategically important launch capabilities, but the reported period does not establish standalone profitability. **UNSOURCED — analyst judgment.**

## Cash Flow, Liquidity, and Capital Structure

Operating activities generated $3,466 of cash during the first six months of 2026, compared with $351 in the prior-year period.[^5] Purchases of property, plant, and equipment were $(28,476), compared with $(6,965).[^5] A simple calculation of operating cash flow less those purchases produces approximately $(25,010) of free cash flow for the first half of 2026.[^5] This is an analyst calculation rather than a company-defined GAAP measure.[^5]

The IPO generated $85,675 of net proceeds, and cash, cash equivalents, and restricted cash ended the period at $94,352.[^2][^5] Total debt and finance leases were $39,364 at June 30, 2026, up from $22,896 at December 31, 2025.[^10] The new SpaceX notes carried an effective interest rate of 6.03%, and the company reported no variable-rate debt outstanding at June 30, 2026.[^11]

The large cash balance provides near-term financial capacity, but the first-half spending rate should not be assumed to be sustainable without a detailed capital plan. **UNSOURCED — analyst judgment.**

## Other Issues Relevant to the Thesis

Customer A represented 17.9% and Customer B represented 12.2% of first-half revenue.[^12] The filing also states that a significant portion of AI infrastructure revenue is concentrated among a small number of customers and that certain cloud-service agreements may be terminated after an initial ramp period with 90 days' notice.[^13]

The company agreed to acquire spectrum licenses for approximately $19.6 billion, consisting of about $11.1 billion of equity consideration and up to $8.5 billion associated with designated EchoStar debt, with any shortfall paid in cash.[^14]

These commitments reinforce the need to evaluate SpaceX on normalized free cash flow and capital allocation rather than revenue growth alone. **UNSOURCED — analyst judgment.**

## Additional Research From Other Filings and Management Commentary

### IPO Prospectus: ARPU Is a Multi-Year Trend

The final IPO prospectus reports Starlink monthly ARPU of $99 in 2023, $91 in 2024, $81 in 2025, and $66 in the first quarter of 2026.[^16] Management stated in the prospectus that it generally expected ARPU to continue declining over the next few years as international subscribers and lower-priced plans became a larger part of the mix, while expecting scale and technological improvements to support revenue growth and lower costs.[^16]

**Why this matters:** The Q2 ARPU decline is part of a longer-running strategy rather than an isolated quarterly result, but the expected cost benefits still need to be demonstrated in future cash flow. **UNSOURCED — analyst interpretation.**

### Post-Quarter 8-K: Cursor Acquisition and Dilution

SpaceX completed its acquisition of Anysphere, the company behind Cursor, on August 14, 2026.[^17] The transaction assigned Cursor an implied equity value of $60.0 billion and converted its outstanding common and preferred shares into approximately 389.3 million SpaceX Class A shares.[^17] SpaceX also assumed or converted approximately 29.1 million restricted stock units and 44.4 million stock options.[^17]

**Why this matters:** The acquisition makes AI integration and acquisition-related dilution more consequential to the investment case than the June 30 balance sheet alone indicates. **UNSOURCED — analyst interpretation.**

### SEC-Filed CEO and CFO Interviews: Long-Term Operating Claims

In SEC-filed pre-IPO interview transcripts, CFO Bret Johnsen said SpaceX expected Starship's eventual rapid reusability to improve cost per kilogram to space by approximately tenfold versus Falcon and said next-generation direct-to-device service was intended to reach 5G-quality performance within two years.[^18] In the same filing, CEO Elon Musk described an initial orbital AI-compute satellite designed for about 120 kilowatts of average power and up to 150 kilowatts at peak.[^18]

**Why this matters:** These statements identify the technical assumptions behind management's vertical-integration thesis, but they are management expectations rather than demonstrated commercial outcomes. **UNSOURCED — analyst interpretation.**

### Q2 Earnings Call: Capital Intensity, Backlog, and Targets

On the August 4 earnings call, management reported $47.5 billion of backlog and approximately $100 billion of cash, cash equivalents, and marketable securities at quarter-end.[^19] Management said Q2 capital expenditures were about $18.4 billion, including approximately $15.8 billion for AI compute infrastructure, and CFO Bret Johnsen expected a similar level of capital spending for the next couple of quarters.[^19] Management also reiterated a target of reaching a $100 billion annualized revenue run rate by December 2026 and expected more than two gigawatts of compute capacity by year-end.[^19]

**Why this matters:** The call strengthens the evidence that high capital spending may persist beyond the reported quarter and provides measurable milestones for backlog conversion, compute deployment, and revenue growth. **UNSOURCED — analyst interpretation.**

## Recent News and Market Expectations

Reuters reported that investors were evaluating whether profits from Connectivity could finance expansion in AI, data centers, and next-generation rockets, while management argued that new AI-compute investments could achieve a payback period of less than one year.[^20] Reuters also reported that the shares fell 7.5% in after-hours trading following the Q2 release after rising 9.4% during the regular session.[^20]

S&P Global Market Intelligence reported that Q2 revenue exceeded Visible Alpha consensus by 14.6% and AI capital expenditures exceeded expectations by 47.5%.[^21] Following the quarter, Visible Alpha's FY2026 revenue consensus increased to $45.9 billion, while its FY2026 AI capital-expenditure estimate increased 39.3%.[^21] These figures are third-party consensus estimates, not company guidance.[^21]

**Why this matters:** Expectations rose after the earnings beat, but investors also required a larger assumed capital commitment; future results must therefore be evaluated against a higher operating and spending bar. **UNSOURCED — analyst interpretation.**

## Consequential Assumptions

- Starlink subscriber growth can offset declining ARPU without materially weakening service quality or unit economics. **UNSOURCED — analyst assumption.**
- Connectivity operating profit is sustainable and can fund part of the investment required by Space and AI. **UNSOURCED — analyst assumption.**
- AI capital spending will eventually produce returns above the fund's cost of capital; current revenue growth alone does not establish this. **UNSOURCED — analyst assumption.**
- First-half 2026 capital spending is elevated rather than a permanent run rate. **UNSOURCED — analyst assumption.**
- Valuation should be anchored to normalized free cash flow because depreciation, share-based compensation, interest, and capital intensity are economically consequential. **UNSOURCED — analyst judgment.**
- Space, Connectivity, and AI can be valued separately with sufficient reliability despite shared infrastructure and corporate costs. **UNSOURCED — analyst assumption.**
- Initiation requires a discount to conservative sum-of-the-parts value and adequate downside protection under slower subscriber growth, continued ARPU pressure, and sustained AI losses. **UNSOURCED — analyst judgment.**

## Research Priorities

The next phase should verify diluted shares, net debt, and enterprise value; build segment forecasts for subscribers, ARPU, launch activity, AI revenue, margins, and capital expenditures; and reconstruct normalized free cash flow under base, upside, and downside scenarios. **UNSOURCED — analyst research plan.**

The valuation work should combine segment-level discounted cash flow analysis with relevant public-company multiples, then test slower subscriber growth, further ARPU compression, launch disruption, persistent AI losses, higher funding costs, and sustained capital intensity. **UNSOURCED — analyst research plan.**
